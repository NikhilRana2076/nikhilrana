02 — Featured research · MSc dissertation 2026

Detecting and Mitigating Hallucinations in Large Language Models for UK iXBRL Financial Reporting

+48.0 pp accuracyp < 0.00112 UK statutory filings102-question benchmark2 LLMs · 3 conditionsWorking demo
i.

The problem

In finance, a confident wrong number is worse than no answer at all.

Banks, auditors, regulators and fintechs want to use LLMs to read company accounts at scale. But when I asked a model straightforward questions about real UK filings, it was wrong more than half the time. It gave fluent, plausible answers with invented figures.

The frustrating part is that UK accounts filed at Companies House already come in iXBRL, where every figure carries a machine-readable tag. Most AI pipelines ignore that structure and treat the filing as plain text.

ii.

The idea

Stop throwing the structure away. Retrieve by tag, not by text.

I built a pipeline that parses iXBRL directly, keeps every fact linked to its XBRL tag, and retrieves by that tag. The model answers from the exact reported figure instead of guessing from nearby text.

iv.

The surprise

0

A popular hallucination detector caught none of the errors.

SelfCheckGPT-style methods flag hallucinations by checking whether a model contradicts itself across repeated answers. On structured financial data, they failed to catch a single baseline error. I diagnosed why. The finding matters for anyone who relies on “self-checking” AI, because in finance, detection has to be grounded in the source data.

v.

Why it matters

Every UK company files accounts, and this approach turns them into something AI can answer questions about reliably. I also built a working demo: upload any UK company filing, inspect its extracted XBRL tags, and question it through Claude Sonnet or GPT side by side.
