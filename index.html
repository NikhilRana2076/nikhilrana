03 — Featured research · MSc dissertation 2026

An AI that reads company accounts by their tags

Every number in a UK company filing already carries a machine-readable label. I built an LLM system that uses it.

UK accounts are filed in iXBRL (Inline XBRL). The document looks like a normal web page to a person, but every figure is wrapped in a tag that says exactly what it is: its accounting concept, period, currency, scale and any breakdown. My system parses those tags into a database and answers questions only from the tagged facts, and if the fact isn’t there, it says so.

Tested on 12 filings (Tesco, Lloyds, AstraZeneca, National Grid and more) · 102-question benchmark · Claude Sonnet 4.6 & GPT-5.6 Terra · Supervised by Dr Ali Gheitasy

A public version of the app is in progress, with sensible usage limits to keep it free. Until then, the assistant below replays real results. In the full app you upload any UK filing, browse its tagged facts and narrative sections, and question it with Claude or GPT.

The key takeaway is that retrieval mainly helped the model answer more (abstentions fell from 52% to 3.9%). The error rate on answered questions changed only modestly (6.1% → 4.1%), so coverage and reliability should be measured separately. The hardest question types gained the most: company-specific tags rose from 15% to 92%, and dimensional questions from 17% to 88%.

Responsible AI

Honest evaluation

Measure coverage and error rates separately, and don’t rely on self-consistency checks when there is more than one true answer.

Data engineering

Structure beats scraping

Parsing the data’s own structure into a clean store did most of the work: 46 → 88 → 94 correct answers.

RegTech & audit

Traceable answers

Every figure links back to the exact tagged fact, period and dimension, which is useful for regulators, auditors and analysts.

ML engineering

Grounding transfers

The same retrieval layer lifted two different frontier models, so the gain came from the data, not one vendor.

Limitations I report openly

  • 12 filings and 102 questions, reduced from the planned 50 and ~500 with supervisor approval.
  • The baseline was cost-constrained to 8,000 tokens of context.
  • Reporting framework and company size are confounded in the corpus.
  • The planned independent dual review couldn’t be completed.

Where I’d take it next

  • Launch the public demo app (in progress, with usage limits).
  • Scale towards 50 filings and 500 questions.
  • Build a detector that checks against all legitimate tagged values.
  • Test a finance-specialised model such as FinGPT or FinMA.
